Coca-Cola price-pack architecture at SuperValu Ireland

A daily, automated read of soft-drink shelf prices that separates regular prices from promotions, benchmarks the full-sugar Coca-Cola premium against Ireland's Sugar-Sweetened Drinks Tax, and compares Coca-Cola with its competitors like-for-like per litre.

Methodology

How the data is collected

Once a day, an automated job reads SuperValu Ireland's public search results for a fixed set of soft-drink and energy-drink queries, at human rate (one page every 3–7 seconds) and without logging in. Each product card yields the price shown, any promotion badge, the pack size and the brand label. Nothing personal is collected.

A run is refused if it returns fewer than half the products of the past week, so a broken or partial page never enters the history. Re-running a day replaces that day's rows rather than duplicating them.

How prices are modelled

  • Price per litre divides the price by the total volume of the pack, so a 12-can pack and a 2 L bottle are comparable.
  • Regular price: on days without a promotion, or with a multibuy, the card shows the shelf price. On price-cut, Rewards-price and value-badge days, the regular price is carried forward from the latest clean day.
  • Price paid after promotions applies the promotional price, or the per-unit price of a multibuy when the offer is taken.
  • Like-for-like comparisons match pack size, pack count, container and sugar tier. Cola comparisons exclude flavoured and caffeine-free variants.

What the numbers do and do not say

The levy ratio compares the full-sugar premium with the tax per litre. It is a benchmark, not a measured pass-through: the premium also reflects each pack's pricing strategy. Shelf prices show three of the four revenue-growth-management levers — price, pack and mix, promotion. Trade investment, volumes and margins are not observable here, and no finding claims them.
